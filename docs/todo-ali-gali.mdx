## Phonetic letters

<LetterTable locale={locale} />

**Final <Mong TODx links="Yp" /> and <Mong TODx links="Ir" /> in letter <Mong TODx links="yX" />**. The written units <Mong TODx links="Yp" /> and <Mong TODx links="Ir" /> record entirely different phonemes and correspond to entirely different letters. The former comes from [U+0FB1 TIBETAN SUBJOINED LETTER YA](https://www.unicode.org/Public/18.0.0/charts/nameslist/0f00/#0fb1) and borrows its glyph directly; the latter is in fact a mirrored <Mong TODx links="Ip" />, just as [U+0F80 TIBETAN VOWEL SIGN REVERSED I](https://www.unicode.org/Public/18.0.0/charts/nameslist/0f00/#0f80) is the mirror of [U+0F72 TIBETAN VOWEL SIGN I](https://www.unicode.org/Public/18.0.0/charts/nameslist/0f00/#0f72). Earlier Chinese national standards did not distinguish the two glyphs, which gave rise to misunderstanding; the revised standard distinguishes the two but still places them under the same character.
