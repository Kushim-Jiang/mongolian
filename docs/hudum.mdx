## Written units

<WrittenUnitTable locale={locale} />

**Written form <Mong MNG links="W" />**. Written form <Mong MNG links="W" /> is used in Hudum letter <Mong MNG links="ee" /> and <Mong MNG links="w" />, but has different origin in these two letters, since the form <Mong MNG links="W" /> in <Mong MNG links="ee" /> comes from Tibetan letter _e_, and the form <Mong MNG links="W" /> in <Mong MNG links="w" /> comes from _beth_.

## Phonetic letters

The variants in gray indicate the fabricated variants, _i.e._ variants borrowed from other joining positions. The variants in yellow indicate the variants that are unattested in the modern orthography of Traditional Mongolian.

<LetterTable locale={locale} />

**Isolated <Mong MNG links="Ix" /> and isolated <Mong MNG links="Ux" />**. Isolated <Mong MNG links="Ix" /> and isolated <Mong MNG links="Ux" /> are used historically, with examples shown in [(Dorji _et al._, 2017)](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2017/17035r2-mongolian-std-var.pdf). In some Traditional Mongolian literature, even if the letter <Mong MNG links="n" /> before isolated <Mong MNG links="Ix" /> or <Mong MNG links="Ux" /> is omitted, the left point is retained and therefore has to be consistently analyzed as belonging to <Mong MNG links="i" />, <Mong MNG links="u" /> or <Mong MNG links="ue" />.

**Final <Mong MNG links="I" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="j" />**. Final <Mong MNG links="I" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="j" /> is introduced in [(Dorji _et al._, 2017)](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2017/17035r2-mongolian-std-var.pdf), where the written form of <Mong MNG links="j" /> in the example <Mong MNG links="b u i – j · a" /> is not final, but isolated. Since there are currently no examples exist to prove its existence, the written form is marked in gray, but the shaping process still supports the theoretical existence of a word with final <Mong MNG links="I" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="j" /> and chachlag.

**Medial <Mong MNG links="I I" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="y" /> and medial <Mong MNG links="O" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="w" />**. Medial <Mong MNG links="I I" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="y" /> and medial <Mong MNG links="O" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="w" /> are introduced in [(Dorji _et al._, 2017)](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2017/17035r2-mongolian-std-var.pdf), where the written form <Mong MNG links="I I" /> of letter <Mong MNG links="y" /> in the examples <Mong MNG links="a y l" /> and <Mong MNG links="a y m a g" />, and the written form <Mong MNG links="O" /> of letter <Mong MNG links="w" /> in the example <Mong MNG links="t a w l a y" /> are all differences derived from letter analysis. Since <Mong MNG links="a i l" />, <Mong MNG links="a i m a g" /> and <Mong MNG links="t a u l a i" /> are preferred, these written forms are marked in gray.

**Letter <Mong MNG links="k2" /> (<Mong MNG links="K2" />) and letter <Mong MNG links="k" /> (<Mong MNG links="K" />)**. Letter <Mong MNG links="k2" /> in form <Mong MNG links="K2" /> is used to represent /kʰ/ (Latin: k) in Hudum literature in China, while letter <Mong MNG links="k" /> in form <Mong MNG links="K" /> is used to represent /kʰ/ (Cyrillic: к) in Hudum literature in Mongolia. The reason for disunifying the two letters is that Hudum Ali Gali uses <Mong MNG links="K2" /> to record /ɡ/ and uses <Mong MNG links="K" /> to record /kʰ/.

**Medial <Mong MNG links="Hr" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="hh" /> and initial <Mong MNG links="Zr" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="zr" />**. Medial <Mong MNG links="Hr" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="hh" /> and initial <Mong MNG links="Zr" /> in letter <Mong MNG links="zr" /> are similar, so they are unified in proposals such as [(SAH, 2017)](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2017/17035r2-mongolian-std-var.pdf). The reason for disunifying the two written units is that Hudum Ali Gali uses <Mong MNG links="Hr" /> to record /ɦ/ and to indicate aspiration, and uses <Mong MNG links="Zr" /> to record /ʈ/.

### Particle mapping

When the nominal spelling of a particle in the table below occurs in text, its letters are reshaped using the `particle` mapping (step 3 in the shaping process above). In the written form, the written units shown in blue are those whose shape is determined by the `particle` condition. A particle prefixed with `mvs` is a standalone particle that follows a Mongolian vowel separator; the preceding MVS is then widened to set it off.
